# article structures

choose the pattern from the reader's question, not the kind of company in the story. blend or reorder
the parts when that makes a better argument; these are not mandatory headings or an excuse to invent
missing evidence. voice remains with [piro-voice](../../voice/SKILL.md).

## technical: how does this work, and should i use it?

- **problem:** describe the concrete failure or constraint and what a useful result would look like.
- **investigation or approach:** walk through the relevant observations, attempted approach, or proposed
  reasoning. distinguish what was tried from what is merely suggested. include failed approaches only
  when supported and useful.
- **concrete example:** work through a supplied input, code snippet, comparison, or reproducible case.
  explain what it demonstrates and what it cannot establish. a hypothetical example stays hypothetical.
- **tradeoffs:** name costs, alternatives, limitations, and conditions under which this approach is wrong.
- **takeaway:** give the reader a bounded conclusion or useful next experiment, not a universal rule.

attach substantive claims to their sources in the outline; keep links and important qualifications
near them in the draft. do not imply a benchmark, tested implementation, or causal result that the
material does not support. a numerically precise example can still be weak evidence.

## product/story: why does this matter, and what changes for me?

- **audience problem:** start with what the reader is trying to do and where the current experience hurts.
  use supported observations, or clearly frame an imagined situation rather than inventing a customer.
- **why it matters:** explain the practical consequence without manufacturing urgency, revenue, or scale.
- **what we built or learned:** tell the relevant change in a useful sequence. use “we” only for supplied
  experience; do not borrow pirog's biography or report an illustrative team as his own.
- **evidence:** show what supports the change or lesson, including uncertainty, counterevidence, and
  limitations. evidence can appear throughout the story instead of in a separate proof section.
- **next step:** leave the reader with a concrete action or open question proportional to the evidence.

the story does not need a triumphant ending. distinguish a decision worth trying from a proven benefit,
and preserve caveats when moving from numbers to narrative. persuasion is not permission to improve
the facts.
